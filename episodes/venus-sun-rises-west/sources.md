## S1 — NASA Science, "Venus: Facts"

URL: https://science.nasa.gov/venus/venus-facts/
Status: **owner re-confirmed 2026-11-25** · retrieved this session.

Exact supporting text:

- "Venus spins slowly in the opposite direction from most planets."
- "For one thing, your 'day' would be 243 Earth days long — longer even than a Venus year (one trip around the Sun), which takes only 225 Earth days."
- "because of the planet's extremely slow rotation, sunrise to sunset would take 117 Earth days."
- "the Sun would rise in the west and set in the east, because Venus spins backward compared to Earth."
- "Its very slight tilt is only three degrees, which is too little to produce noticeable seasons."
- "The atmosphere is mostly carbon dioxide … The atmospheric pressure at the surface measures a crushing 93 times what it is at sea level on Earth."

Notes:
- S1's prose says "sunrise to sunset would take 117 Earth days". S3 publishes the **full solar day** as 116.75 Earth days. For the airtight closing line, the script uses the S3 value: half of 116.75 ≈ **58 days** → "nearly two months". S1 is retained for the west/east sunrise and 243/225 contrast.
- S1 describes the tilt as "only three degrees"; S3 publishes **177.36°** under the retrograde convention and S4 lists **177.3°**. These are not relabelled as each other. Narration says "177-degree tilt"; any on-screen chip uses S3's `177.36°`.

## S3 — NASA NSSDCA, "Venus Fact Sheet"

URL: https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/venusfact.html
Status: **owner re-confirmed 2026-11-25** · retrieved this session.

Exact table values used:

- `Sidereal orbit period (days)` — **224.701**
- `Sidereal rotation period (hrs)` — **−5832.6** (negative = retrograde)
- `Length of day (hrs)` — **2802.0**, Earth ratio **116.750**
- `Obliquity to orbit (deg)` — **177.36**
- `Inclination of equator (deg)` — **2.64**
- `Surface pressure: 92 bars`
- Atmospheric composition near surface: **96.5% CO₂**

Notes:
- The negative sidereal rotation plus the 177.36° obliquity support "flipped nearly upside down" and "rotating clockwise".
- `116.750` is the full solar day; the closing "nearly two months" is `116.75 / 2 ≈ 58.4` Earth days. This is the airtight derivation used by the script.

## S4 — Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Venus"

URL: https://www.britannica.com/place/Venus-planet
Status: **owner re-confirmed 2026-11-25** · retrieved this session (previous Kids URL retired).

Exact supporting text:

- "The Sun and most of the planets in the solar system rotate in a counterclockwise direction when viewed from above their north poles; this direction is called direct, or prograde. Venus, however, rotates in the opposite, or retrograde, direction."
- "Were it not for the planet's clouds, an observer on Venus's surface would see the Sun rise in the west and set in the east."
- "The idea cited most often is that, when Venus was forming from the accretion of planetary building blocks (planetesimals), one of the largest of these bodies collided with the proto-Venus in such a way as to tip it over and possibly slow its spin as well."
- "Astronomers as yet have no satisfactory explanation for Venus's peculiar rotational characteristics."
- Quick facts: "rotation period (Venusian sidereal day) 243 Earth days (retrograde)", "Venusian mean solar day 116.8 Earth days", "inclination of equator to orbit 177.3°", "surface pressure at mean radius 95 bars".

Notes:
- Supports the "almost every other planet … upright top" line and the giant-impact line.
- "Astronomers as yet have no satisfactory explanation" is why the script says "Scientists think" and "likely" instead of stating the cause as settled.

## S5 — Correia & Laskar, "The four final rotation states of Venus", Nature 411, 767–770 (2001)

URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/35081000
Status: **owner-opened 2026-11-25** · retrieved this session.

Exact abstract text used:

- "Venus rotates very slowly on its axis in a retrograde direction, opposite to that of most other bodies in the Solar System."
- "it has been generally believed that in the past its rotational axis was itself rotated to 180° as a result of core–mantle friction inside the planet, together with atmospheric tides."
- "terrestrial planets with dense atmosphere like Venus can evolve into one of only four possible rotation states."
- "most initial conditions will drive the planet towards the configuration at present seen at Venus, albeit through two very different evolutionary paths."
- "it is possible for Venus to begin with prograde rotation … yet then develop retrograde rotation while the obliquity goes towards zero."

Notes:
- Supports the atmospheric-tide / dense-atmosphere brake and the long-term reversal framing.
- Does **not** support the giant impact; that claim is mapped to S4.
